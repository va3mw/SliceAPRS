SliceAPRS 

      
SliceAPRS listens to APRS on 2 m through a FlexRadio and uses the packets to spot 2 m path openings (tropo and other enhancements) for 2 m DX. APRS stations beacon their positions all the time, so they make a dense network of 2 m beacons: when the band opens, you start to hear stations directly from much farther away than usual.
        Source and releases: github.com/va3mw/SliceAPRS

      
Receive only: SliceAPRS never transmits. SmartSDR and DAX do not need to be running.

    


    

      

        
Getting started

        
    
          
  1. Set up the radio for 2 m: a FLEX-6700 covers it; a FLEX-6600 or other model needs a 2 m transverter in the radio's XVTR settings.
    2. 
          
  2. In Settings, enter your callsign and grid. Click Find radios and pick yours, or type its IP address.
    3. 
          
  3. Check the frequency (144.390 MHz in North America, 144.800 MHz in Europe), the receive antenna, and a DAX IQ channel nobody else uses.
    4. 
          
  4. Press Save and restart. SliceAPRS opens its own panadapter and DAX IQ stream; packets appear in Packets.
    5. 
          
  5. Leave Openings up on the desktop. Your normal range needs a day or two of listening to learn.
    6. 
        

      

      

        
Who was heard directly

        
Each packet carries its digipeater path, and each digipeater that repeats it sets a "has-been-repeated" bit. The signal that reached your antenna came from the last digipeater with that bit set, or from the sender if no digipeater has repeated it yet.

        
    
          
  • A relayed packet is measured to the digipeater, never to the original sender. Digipeater positions are learned from their own beacons.
    • 
          
  • Not counted: packets from the internet (third-party, TCPIP), satellite and ISS paths (and the 145.825 MHz channel), packets repeated by a digipeater that did not add its call, and your own callsign.
    • 
          
  • Positions: objects and items do not move their sender. A position that jumps too far, too fast is held until a second report agrees; the Packets tab shows both the station's last position and the one in the packet.
    • 
        

      

      

        
How openings are detected

        
Normal range, for each UTC hour of the day: the 90th percentile of each station's farthest direct distance per hour over the last 14 days (the hour either side counts too). Until each hour has enough history, all hours together are used; with almost no history, only the distance rule works. Stations heard directly on 3 or more days are your usual stations.

        
In the last 30 minutes, a far station is one heard directly beyond normal × the sensitivity factor, and not one of your usual stations.

        
    
          
  • Enhanced: at least the sensitivity's number of far stations (1 at medium).
    • 
          
  • Open: more far stations (3 at medium), or any direct path over the set distance (300 km to start).
    • 
        

        
An opening starts with its first far packet and ends at the last one, once 30 minutes pass with none. Its direction is the average bearing of the far stations; its strength rates how many there were and how far beyond normal.

        

      

      

        
The tabs

        

          
Openings
Status now, the farthest direct stations, a map and polar plot of the direct paths, the last 24 hours against normal, and the log of openings.

          
Stations
Every station heard, with its last position, distance, bearing and whether it was heard directly.

          
Grids
Every 4-character grid square heard directly, on a grid map and in a table, for the last day, week or all time.

          
Packets
Every packet, raw and decoded, with the station heard directly marked.

          
Settings
Station, radio, frequency, antenna, DAX IQ channel, opening rules, web page and data folder.

          
Log
What SliceAPRS is doing, and the decoder counts.

        

        
The map needs internet for its tiles; without it only the plots are shown. History (packets, paths, openings, stations) is kept in the data folder and is never deleted by SliceAPRS.
